# AMC v1.7.0 — Phone Control

The agent can now use your phone the way you do: read the screen, open apps, tap, type and scroll, look at screenshots, and read and answer notifications. You always see when it does, you can stop it at once, and everything that matters still asks you first.

Phone control is off until you switch it on in **Setup → Phone control**.

## What the agent can do

- **Read the screen** as a short list of elements with ids, such as `[e12] Button "Send" (click)`. Password fields never show their content.
- **Operate apps:** tap (by element id, or by position as a fallback), type, scroll, swipe, long-press, and press Back, Home, Recents, the notification shade and quick settings. It can open apps by name, list installed apps, and wait for something to appear.
- **Check its own work:** every action returns the new screen, so the agent sees whether it worked before the next step. After three failed attempts at the same step it stops and tells you.
- **Screenshots for vision models:** on Android 11 and later through the accessibility service. On Android 8 to 10 through screen capture, after Android's own consent dialog. Offered only when your model can look at images. Protected windows (FLAG_SECURE) give no image.
- **Notifications:** list them, open them, reply directly (WhatsApp, Telegram, SMS and others), and dismiss them. One-time codes are hidden. Optionally: "when a notification from app X arrives, run task Y" in the background.
- **System features without special rights:** alarms and timers, a prefilled calendar event (you save it), the share sheet, web links, common settings pages, the clipboard, and screen time (with usage access).
- **Optional Shizuku:** `privileged_shell` runs `input`, `pm`, `settings`, `dumpsys`, `screencap` and `am` with ADB rights, without root. Everything else works without it.

The agent still prefers Termux when a command does the job just as well. Screen control is for apps without an API.

## You stay in control

- **Visible and stoppable:** while the agent operates the phone, an "AMC is in control" banner with a **Stop** button stays on top, and an ongoing notification has a **Stop** action. Stop ends the task at once, in the app, in Telegram conversations and in background tasks.
- **Always asks, in every mode (including Turbo):** sending messages (also by tapping "Send" in WhatsApp, Telegram, e-mail and similar apps), payments and purchases, installing and uninstalling apps, deleting data, logins and account changes, calls, the camera, and changes in system settings. Taps whose target AMC cannot verify in a messenger also ask.
- **Private apps:** reading a messenger, contacts or the call history asks once per app and task. Sending still asks every time.
- **Block list:** banking and payment apps, password managers, authenticator apps, Google Play payment dialogs and the security pages of the system settings are blocked by default. Apps with such names are blocked too. You can release single apps in **Settings → Phone control**, and block more.
- **Never:** AMC never unlocks or bypasses the lock screen, never operates itself, and never types into terminal apps (that would bypass the command filter).
- **Telegram:** screen control is off by default. When you switch it on, every screen action comes as an approval button, and screenshots are only sent to the chat when you ask for one.
- **Audit log:** every action is recorded. Typed passwords are never stored in the chat, the audit log or the model's context.

## Setup

- A second checklist group, **Phone control**, with one action per step: allow restricted settings (Android 13 and later), turn on AMC Phone Control, allow notification access, and the optional usage access and Shizuku.
- **Android 13 and later:** because AMC comes from GitHub and not from a store, Android blocks accessibility and notification access until you allow it. The checklist explains the path (**App info → ⋮ → Allow restricted settings**) and opens App info for you.
- Before Android's accessibility settings open, a short page explains what the service reads and does. Nothing is preselected; "Not now" is as easy as continuing.

## Settings

- **Phone control** card: what is on, switches for Telegram and background tasks (both off by default), the block list, and your notification triggers.
- **Try a command** now also covers the new tools, for example `screen_tap Send @com.whatsapp` or `open_app Sparkasse`, and shows a separate Telegram row.

## Under the hood

- New package `com.agent.mobile.phone`: `ScreenTreeSerializer`, `ElementMatcher`, `PhoneToolPolicy`, `PackageBlocklist`, `PrivilegedCommandPolicy` and the notification and intent helpers are plain Kotlin and unit-tested. `PhoneControlService` (accessibility), `AmcNotificationListener`, `ScreenCapture` and `ShizukuShell` connect them to Android.
- The engine talks to the phone through the `PhoneController` interface, so the agent loop stays testable. Per-run approvals for private apps, the REMOTE rule and redaction of typed passwords are covered by engine tests.
- New unit tests: 121 (272 in total). `lint` reports no errors.
- New permissions: `QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES` (open apps by name), `SET_ALARM`, `PACKAGE_USAGE_STATS` (only used when you grant usage access), `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PROJECTION` (screenshots on Android 8 to 10). The accessibility service and notification access are only active after you switch them on.

## Not checked on a device

This release was built and unit-tested, but the accessibility service, gestures, the overlay, screenshots, notification replies and Shizuku were not exercised on a real phone. Please report anything that does not work as described.
